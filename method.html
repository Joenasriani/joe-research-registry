Research Method

The registry records a question only after separating the wording being proposed from concepts that already exist.

01 / Define

The first task is to state one question precisely enough that its assumptions can be challenged. Terms that change the scope of the question are defined before conclusions are drawn.

02 / Search

Searches use the proposed wording and neighboring terminology. The purpose is not to prove novelty by failing to find an identical phrase. It is to identify the closest prior theories, empirical cases, competing explanations, and earlier formulations.

03 / Separate

Each note distinguishes three things: what prior literature already establishes, what the current note is proposing, and what remains uncertain. Similarity in language is not treated as identity of concept, and different language is not treated as proof of originality.

04 / Source

Primary literature, academic publishers, research archives, and direct institutional sources are preferred where available. Sources are linked so a reader can inspect the underlying work rather than rely on the registry's summary.

05 / Revise

Publication does not freeze a note. A later source may narrow the claim, reveal an older precedent, change a definition, or overturn the framing. Git history keeps those changes visible.
