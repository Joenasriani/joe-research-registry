Research Note 001 Working question Joe Nasr 10 September 2026

Pre Institutional Human Order

If government and every other deliberately designed institution of collective order are removed from the model, what forms of human coordination would still arise from interaction itself?

Closest established research: spontaneous order, order without government, stateless societies, self governance, informal institutions, political anthropology, and self organization.

01 / The question

Asking what happens without government does not remove human systems of order.

A society without a state can still contain law, markets, custom, religious authority, councils, property rules, leadership, exchange systems, kinship obligations, and enforcement. Those arrangements can perform functions usually associated with government.

This note changes the starting condition. It removes government and then removes the wider layer of deliberately designed institutions that could simply replace it. The question is what coordination appears before that layer is available.

02 / The subtraction test

  1. Remove government as formal authority.
  2. Remove deliberately designed institutions that could substitute for government.
  3. Keep biology, kinship, memory, communication, scarcity, geography, conflict, reciprocity, imitation, trust, fear, reputation, and repeated interaction.
  4. Identify coordination that can arise from those remaining conditions.
  5. Separate early emergent patterns from later rules, roles, offices, and institutions.

This is a conceptual test. It does not assert that any known human population has existed in a perfectly institution free condition.

03 / Three levels

Natural constraint. Bodies require food, rest, protection, reproduction, and access to material resources. Geography and scarcity shape behavior before formal rules are written.

Emergent social coordination. Repeated interaction can produce trust, retaliation, reciprocity, reputation, imitation, status, leadership, alliances, territorial behavior, and shared expectations without a single designer.

Constructed institutional order. Roles, procedures, offices, codified rules, recognized authorities, ownership systems, bureaucracies, and enforcement structures can stabilize or redirect earlier patterns.

The difficult boundary is between the second and third levels. When does an emergent pattern become an institution?

04 / Closest literature

This note overlaps with established research but does not treat those fields as newly discovered.

Spontaneous order Explains how practices and institutions can result from human action without being centrally designed. This is the closest theoretical neighbor.
Order without government Examines how societies coordinate without centralized state authority. Such societies can still possess dense systems of custom, leadership, obligation, dispute settlement, and exchange.
Stateless societies Studies social and political organization in the absence of a state. Stateless does not mean institutionless.
Self governance Examines rule production and coordination outside or below the state. It can still involve explicit rules, organizations, and enforcement.
Self organization Studies patterned order produced by local interaction without a single controlling agent. It provides mechanisms that may help explain social emergence.

05 / What is distinct here

The proposed move is the subtraction test, not the claim that order can exist without government.

The comparison is therefore not government versus anarchy. It is deliberately constructed institutional order versus coordination that appears before deliberate institutional construction.

The next problem follows immediately. If recurring coordination hardens into expectations, roles, sanctions, and authority, then constructed order may be less a separate category than a later stage in the same process.

06 / Questions to test

  1. Which forms of coordination can arise from repeated interaction without explicit rule design?
  2. Which patterns recur across unrelated cultures?
  3. Which patterns require language, memory, kinship, property, territory, or repeated exchange?
  4. What is the smallest condition needed for authority to appear?
  5. When does reputation become norm, norm become rule, and rule become institution?
  6. Can a stable social order remain noninstitutional, or does stability itself create institutions?

07 / Direct answers

Can social order exist without government?

Yes. Research on spontaneous order, stateless societies, customary organization, and nonstate governance examines coordination outside centralized government. The existence of order without government is therefore not the novel claim in this note.

What creates social order without central authority?

Depending on the setting, coordination can arise through repeated interaction, communication, reciprocity, reputation, kinship, custom, local sanctions, exchange, coalition formation, and shared expectations. Some of these can later become institutions.

Is a stateless society the same as an institutionless society?

No. A stateless society can have rules, authority, norms, property arrangements, dispute settlement, kinship systems, and other institutions. This distinction is central to the subtraction test.

What does Pre Institutional Human Order mean in this registry?

It is a working label for the layer of human coordination being examined after deliberately designed institutions are removed from the starting model. It is not presented as an established academic field.

How is this different from spontaneous order?

Spontaneous order studies outcomes and institutions that emerge without central design. This note asks an earlier boundary question: what remains after designed institutions are removed, and which emergent patterns appear before they harden into institutions?
