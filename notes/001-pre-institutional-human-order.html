Research Note 001 Working question Joe Nasr 10 September 2026

Pre Institutional Human Order

If government and every other deliberately designed institution of collective order are removed from the model, what forms of human coordination would still arise from interaction itself?

01 / The question

The usual question asks what happens without government. This note asks a stricter question.

Removing government alone leaves many other forms of constructed order in place. Law, markets, formal religion, corporations, councils, organized exchange, property rules, customary institutions, and other arrangements can continue to coordinate people even when a state is absent.

The subtraction test removes that wider institutional layer from the starting model. The question then becomes: what forms of coordination appear before a deliberately designed system is available to organize them?

02 / The subtraction test

  1. Remove government as a formal authority.
  2. Remove other deliberately designed institutions that could simply replace government.
  3. Do not remove biology, kinship, memory, communication, scarcity, geography, conflict, reciprocity, imitation, trust, fear, reputation, or repeated interaction.
  4. Observe what forms of coordination would be expected to arise from those remaining conditions.
  5. Separate the first emergent patterns from later institutions built on top of them.

This is a conceptual test, not a claim that a human population has ever existed in a perfectly institution free condition.

03 / Three levels

Natural constraint. Bodies require food, rest, protection, reproduction, and access to physical resources. Geography and material limits shape what people can do before any rule is written.

Emergent social coordination. Repeated interaction can produce trust, retaliation, reciprocity, reputation, imitation, status, leadership, alliances, territorial behavior, and shared expectations without a central designer.

Constructed institutional order. Roles, procedures, offices, codified rules, recognized authorities, formal ownership systems, bureaucracies, and enforcement structures can later stabilize or redirect those earlier patterns.

The research question sits at the boundary between the second and third levels. At what point does an emergent pattern become an institution?

04 / Prior literature

This question touches several established bodies of work. None of them should be relabelled as new.

Spontaneous order Studies how practices and institutions can arise from many individual actions without being centrally designed. This is the closest established concept, but it often includes institutions that have already emerged.
Stateless societies Studies social and political organization without a state. Stateless does not mean institutionless. Kinship rules, customary authority, exchange systems, councils, and norms may still be highly organized.
Self governance Examines coordination and rule production outside or below the state. It can still involve explicit organizations, rules, and enforcement.
Customary organization Shows that property, dispute resolution, and authority can be supplied through nonstate arrangements. These arrangements are still institutions.
Complexity and self organization Studies patterned order that develops from local interaction without a single controlling agent. This supplies mechanisms that may help explain social emergence.

05 / What is distinct here

The claim is not that order can exist without government. The distinct move is to remove the broader category of deliberately constructed institutional order before asking what remains.

That changes the comparison. The contrast is no longer government versus anarchy. It becomes designed institutional order versus coordination that arises before deliberate institutional construction.

A second question follows immediately: if emergent coordination repeatedly hardens into rules, roles, and authority, is constructed order a separate category or a later phase of the same process?

06 / Questions to test

  1. Which forms of coordination can arise from repeated interaction without explicit rule design?
  2. Which of those patterns appear across unrelated cultures?
  3. Which require language, memory, kinship, property, territory, or repeated exchange?
  4. What is the smallest condition needed for authority to appear?
  5. When does reputation become norm, norm become rule, and rule become institution?
  6. Can a stable social order remain permanently noninstitutional, or does stability itself create institutions?
